That has allowed me to move from AI-assisted productivity into AI-native product development.

## 3. Best Website Version — Longer Story

### From Using AI to Orchestrating It

I started using AI the way most executives did: research, writing, analysis, brainstorming, presentations, and faster problem solving. But the more I used it, the more I realized the real opportunity was not asking one AI to do more work. It was learning how to make **different AIs work together as a system**.

I began treating AI models like specialized members of a team. One agent researches. Another designs the architecture. Another writes code. Another challenges assumptions. Another reviews implementation. GitHub preserves the work, decisions, handoffs, branches, issues, tests, and progress. Rules and reusable skills make the process repeatable rather than dependent on one conversation.

That approach eventually became PARKOUR, a cross-project AI operating framework I developed to coordinate standards, workflows, agent roles, research patterns, engineering practices, progress tracking, GitHub handoffs, and continuous execution across multiple software projects.

Today I am using that environment to develop projects ranging from enterprise retail software to real-time performance technology. I am not trying to replace engineers with prompts. I am building a new kind of operating capability: combining domain expertise, product judgment, AI orchestration, existing software, automated research, and AI-assisted engineering to move from idea to tested implementation dramatically faster.

My advantage is the combination. I understand how businesses actually operate, how margin is created or lost, how products move from concept to shelf, how systems break at scale — and increasingly, how to translate those operating requirements into software using AI-native development methods.
