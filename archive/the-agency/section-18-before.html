Adaptive Audio Engine

ATOMIQ

LIVE 1.0 · ADAPTIVE AUDIO ENGINE
The idea behind the work

Music is another place where I like figuring out how the pieces connect. ATOMIQ began as a browser-based DJ application and grew into a performance system organized around one shared musical clock.

The challenge is to keep audio, hardware and visuals working together while leaving the performance decisions with the DJ. It is an example of how I map a complicated live workflow into connected tools with a clear human operator.

Inside the work
  • Started as a real DJ app running in the browser — four decks, real audio, MIDI hardware support, track analysis, and saved libraries — and grew into ATOMIQ LIVE 1.0, a complete performance system.
  • The Adaptive Audio Engine is the system's shared clock: decks, waveforms, stems, hardware lights, and visuals all stay in time with the music's phrase, key, and energy. The software spots the moment; the DJ makes the call.
  • Tested like serious software: hardware control passed 400/400 checks three runs in a row, the interface 489/489, and the intelligence layer 433/433 — with mix scoring proven against a 5,000-track library.
  • The intelligence picks the best next track, explains exactly how to mix into it, and can even mix on its own — but the moment the DJ touches a fader, control snaps back instantly. It helps the performer, never replaces them.
  • The product site, the live browser booth (desktop and phone), hardware connections, video output for the crowd, and one unified music library were all built with AI as the tools (Grok + Claude) — directed like a product team, not generated as a mockup.
How it comes together
  1. 01

    Business problem

    DJs needed audio that adapts to the music without taking away control

  2. 02

    Studied existing DJ software, browser audio limits, and MIDI hardware standards

  3. 03

    Product definition

    Scoped LIVE 1.0: four decks, stems, hardware link, and the CORE engine

  4. 04

    Architecture

    Designed the Adaptive Audio Engine as one shared clock for every surface

  5. 05

    Parallel AI agents

    Grok and Claude built decks, mixer, library, and visuals side by side

  6. 06

    GitHub — source of truth

    Every module, test log, and handoff lived in one repo

  7. 07

    Build & integration

    Combined decks, sampler, sequencer, and mobile into one live booth

  8. 08

    Tests / review

  9. 09

    Human gates

    Nothing shipped until it felt right on real hardware, by hand

  10. 10

    Iteration / product

    Shipped LIVE 1.0; mix scoring proven against a 5,000-track library

Retail OTB, Sourcing & Private-Label Intelligence Platform

MERCHANT PRO

IN DEVELOPMENT
The idea behind the work

Years of buying, sourcing and managing margin kept leading back to the same question: how does a retailer connect a product opportunity with its cash, stock and selling plan? MERCHANT PRO brings those decisions into one operating model.

I began with a wider retail platform, then narrowed the first product around Open-to-Buy, sourcing, private label and landed cost. The purpose is to help a buying team see the economic consequences of its choices before committing inventory dollars.

Inside the work
  • Designed a 21-module retail operating platform, then narrowed the first standalone product to Open-to-Buy, sourcing, private label, landed cost, and product-opportunity analysis.
  • Defined an OTB model that separates retail OTB from cost OTB, including a 30%-to-75% margin scenario that releases $4,500 of purchasing capacity on $10,000 of sales.
  • Built the TypeScript/Node shared financial kernel with bigint Money and Quantity value objects plus 36 passing unit and property tests.
  • Researched a provider-agnostic Phase-1 stack for retail price, supplier discovery, freight, and HTS/duty data using SerpApi, ImportYeti, Freightos, USITC, and optional commercial adapters.
  • Established Google Sheets/CSV/XLSX as the low-friction data path while preserving later ERP/POS/API integration with Oracle, SAP, NetSuite, Dynamics, Shopify, Lightspeed, and Square.

AI-Assisted Pickleball Intelligence & Training

PINPOINT

EXPLORATION / PROTOTYPE
The idea behind the work

My interest in pickleball led to a practical question: can a player understand why a point changed, then practice the right response? PINPOINT explores that connection between seeing, understanding and improving.

The concept links match capture and analysis to plain-language guidance and targeted training. The transferable principle is a useful learning loop: collect evidence, make it understandable and connect it to the next action.

Inside the work
  • Connects match capture, court calibration, movement analysis, plain-language explanations, and targeted training in one improvement loop.
  • Uses Court IQ scenarios to teach positioning, recovery, spacing, shot geometry, and the reason a point changed.
  • Builds Match Intelligence from player location, shot patterns, kitchen-line behavior, partner spacing, winners, errors, and pressure moments.
  • Develops a multidimensional player model across serve, return, dink, volley, positioning, reaction, consistency, power, accuracy, strategy, and awareness.
  • Extends the same intelligence layer into player profiles, equipment guidance, combine testing, and repeatable practice scenarios.

The idea behind the work

I structured the work around defined services, numbered stages and clear handoffs. The organization gains a way to track responsibility and progress, while the client gets a clearer experience of what happens next.

Inside the work
  • Runs every client job through ten numbered stages, 01 Intake to 10 Delivery, and the client only ever sees those two.
  • A 12-service rate card (research through media buying) prices work once; clients tick services at intake without seeing rates.
  • One board tracks many projects at once, each with per-stage progress bars, due-date countdowns, a project ID convention and an archive.
  • The video variant budgets each stage in time and money — about $675 of AI generation per finished video across script, stills, motion, sound and up-res.

Unified Multi-Channel Point of Sale System

UNIT POS

INTERACTIVE PROTOTYPE
The idea behind the work

A café, a shop and a bar share the need to take an accurate order quickly, but their products and selling details differ. UNIT POS explores how one register can adapt to those different operating contexts.

I connected multiple ways of entering an item with catalogs and modifiers suited to each business type. The aim is practical consistency: a system that follows the work at the counter and gives the wider business usable transaction information.

Inside the work
  • One register serves three business types — café, retail, bar — switched from a single home-screen dropdown, each with its own catalog and modifier sets.
  • Items enter an order five ways: tap grid, name/SKU search, barcode scan, PLU keypad, and weight scale, sized for catalogs of hundreds of items.
  • Payment covers card, cash with change calculation, gift card and house account, splits up to 4 ways, and 4 tip presets.
  • The product database syncs from a Google Sheet, and tabs, refunds and end-of-day treat counter and web-store sales as one ledger.
  • One codebase renders both target devices, iPad (1194×834) and phone (390×844), with a persistent sync strip for Sheet, payments, web store, kitchen and offline status.
