### Context
I established More Margin Co. as a way to put longstanding relationships and operating experience to work on specific business needs. Suppliers come to me to explore new retail channels; retailers need help with assortments, stores, and the systems behind them. My contribution is connecting the commercial question with what it takes to deliver.

The DJI Fifth Avenue flagship was an eight-month engagement spanning 2025–2026, beginning after site selection. We built financial models to assess the store both on its own and as part of STC’s existing business. The standalone case showed limited profitability, but the projected benefit to the wider business made a stronger case for proceeding.

### The Work
Once the DJI project cleared that gate, I worked through the lease and build process with the team. Using specifications from DJI’s China headquarters, I developed the layout, merchandising plan, training processes, and replenishment models covering supply from China and the New Jersey warehouse. We obtained contractor bids, carried out the build, and integrated the operation with STC’s customer, warehouse, product-information, and accounting systems.

NuvoMed presented a different opportunity. I had worked with its parent company for more than eight years on sourcing and importing products. With national retail distribution already established, they asked me to help find additional markets. We identified outdoor retail, drawing on my experience and relationships in that sector. I later helped develop a drive-aisle PDQ display program to support their expansion at Home Depot and Lowe’s.

### Outcome
The DJI store opened around February–March 2026 after receiving its New York City certificate of occupancy and approval from China headquarters, with the operating processes and systems connections in place. I remain a contact for STC when similar complex projects arise.

For NuvoMed, our work brought products into Fun Town RV, Camping World, REI, and other retailers. The home-improvement display program created another route to expand the offer. I estimate that the combined work contributed more than $5 million in revenue over ten months. The relationship continues, with the company returning to me as new needs arise.

### Takeaway
A store opening or a new retail placement also needs useful information behind it. Alongside assortment work—what to carry, how to price it, and what to stop buying—I have built dashboards for small businesses and e-commerce operations, bringing scattered data into a form stakeholders can access and understand.

I approach the analysis with confirmation bias in mind: a dashboard should help people examine what is happening, including results that challenge their expectations. Across these engagements, the aim is to leave people with an operation, a sales channel, or a practical tool they can use.

Alongside client engagements, I continually develop my own tools to address recurring business needs and make them available as part of future client work. These are ongoing product-development projects, separate from client deployments.
